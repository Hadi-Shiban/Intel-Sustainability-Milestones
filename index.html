A cinematic timeline

The story of innovation, responsibility, and what comes next.

1968

Two founders. One idea. A future waiting to be built.

Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore establish Intel, laying the foundation for decades of semiconductor innovation.

Every future begins with the people who imagine it.

1971

A computer's power begins to fit on a single chip.

Intel introduces the 4004, the world's first commercial microprocessor.

Smaller technology opens the door to possibilities that reach far beyond the laboratory.

1978

An architecture built to last.

The 8086 establishes the x86 architecture that powers countless PCs and servers.

Enduring ideas connect generations of technology and the people who depend on it.

1985

More power. More possibilities.

The 386 introduces 32-bit architecture and expands performance and multitasking for personal computers.

Computing becomes part of how families learn, work, and connect.

2006

Progress brings responsibility.

Intel reaches its highest annual greenhouse gas emissions for operations, followed by investments in abatement, renewable energy, and more efficient manufacturing.

Building the future also means confronting the environmental impact of how we build it.

2020

A broader definition of progress.

Intel launches its Responsible, Inclusive, Sustainable, Enabling strategy and 2030 goals.

Responsible innovation considers people, communities, water, waste, and climate together.

2022

A commitment for the decades ahead.

Intel announces its goal of net-zero Scope 1 and 2 greenhouse gas emissions across global operations by 2040.

The choices made today shape the world future generations will inherit.

2023

Progress becomes measurable.

Intel achieves 99% renewable electricity usage worldwide, according to the supplied assignment content.

Cleaner energy is one part of reducing the footprint of technological progress.

2024

The next chapter is built together.

Intel hosts its first Sustainability Summit, bringing together suppliers, government officials, and industry leaders.

Lasting progress depends on collaboration across communities and industries.
